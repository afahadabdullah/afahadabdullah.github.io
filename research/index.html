Origins

Doctoral research

My Ph.D. (George Mason University, 2020, advised by Dr. Natalie Burls) investigated what controls the strength, seasonality, and future of subtropical anticyclones — the great high-pressure systems over the subtropical oceans that shape rainfall, drought, and marine climate in both hemispheres. Using CESM experiments, CMIP5/6 ensembles, and dynamical diagnostics, I showed how tropical diabatic heating, direct CO₂ radiative forcing, and indirect SST warming each imprint on the anticyclones and the storm tracks — mechanisms that now inform my model bias-correction work at NASA. This program also contributed to the Cape Town "Day Zero" drought study and emergent-constraint assessments of CMIP6 circulation projections.
